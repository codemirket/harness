# Positioning

Identify the intended segment, buying/use situation and alternatives actually considered. Inspect customer language, product capabilities and competitor evidence with current dates. Separate desirable aspirations from claims the product can substantiate.

Define the category/context that helps the audience understand the offer, the distinct value and why it matters in that situation. Link differentiators to proof and state the conditions under which they hold. Avoid unsupported superlatives, invented traction and comparison claims based on a competitor's old behavior.

Translate the choice into a message hierarchy: primary promise, supporting benefits, proof and appropriate next action. Keep wording coherent across the intended surface without forcing every possible audience into one statement.

Return a positioning statement, claim/evidence map, message hierarchy and unresolved assumptions. Stop when the audience and defensible difference are clear enough for copy, or identify the missing evidence/decision. Do not publish or alter campaign accounts from a positioning brief.
