# Executive strategy

Help the owner make a consequential choice. A request for a CEO perspective calls
for reasoning across the business, not roleplay, invented authority or an expanded
mandate to make commitments. Scale the work to the decision; a short choice need
not become a strategy deck.

## Establish what can change

Identify the decision, owner, horizon, desired business outcome and binding
constraints. Reuse existing commitments and owner decisions. Separate facts,
estimates and desired targets; trace material customer, revenue and cost claims to
their sources and dates. Product features and a pricing page do not establish
demand, willingness to pay or collected revenue.

Describe the customer segment, problem, current alternative, reason to choose the
offer and plausible route to reach buyers. Test the strongest competing explanation
for demand: one sponsor, a temporary subsidy or acquisition mix can masquerade as
repeatable pull. Use customer research when evidence is missing; do not invent a
market size or contact customers merely to complete the analysis.

## Compare real alternatives

Compare viable options, including maintaining the current course or a bounded
test. For each material option, connect:

- **Customer and strategic fit:** whose outcome improves, evidence of demand,
  differentiated value, and what the choice deliberately leaves out.
- **Economics:** revenue mechanism, incremental contribution and acquisition,
  delivery, support and retention costs. Define units, period and exclusions.
- **Cash and commitments:** timing of collections and payments, working capital,
  committed obligations, liquidity floor and downside exposure. Booked revenue,
  accounting profit and available cash are different quantities.
- **Capacity and opportunity cost:** scarce people, lead times, dependencies and
  the work displaced. A positive forecast does not create delivery capacity.
- **Reversibility and concentration:** exit cost, customer/channel dependency,
  contractual exposure and which uncertainty a smaller step could resolve.

For consequential resource choices, read
[the worked allocation decision](references/allocation-decision.md). Build a small
reproducible calculation with explicit assumptions when numbers could change the
answer. Reconcile units and avoid double counting existing payroll, revenue or
shared costs. A cash-runway ratio is insufficient when payments are lumpy; use the
relevant collection/payment schedule. Check a downside and a decision-changing
threshold, rather than presenting a precise point forecast as certainty. Do not
assign scenario probabilities without support or hide a hard constraint in a
weighted score. Use data-analysis for source transformations and experiment-design
for a causal test when those capabilities are available.

## Recommend a choice and an operating test

Lead with the supported choice, decisive tradeoff, strongest objection and what
would change the recommendation. Name any assumption that could reverse it and
the cheapest useful evidence to resolve that assumption. Distinguish a strategic
choice from a list of aspirations; say which competing work should wait and why.

Turn the choice into the next coherent step with an owner, resource envelope,
dependencies, review point and proposed expand/stop conditions. Separate leading
signals from realized business outcomes and preserve important guardrails. Missing
baselines stay missing; proposed targets are proposals, not measured results or
new business commitments. Use the existing planning record and product workflow
for implementation details.

Make the authority boundary explicit where consequential: recommendation,
approved allocation, contractual commitment and executed action are separate.
Reuse authorization already given; do not repeatedly ask for permission or treat
this skill as authority to hire, spend, publish prices or contact third parties.
End with the decision the owner actually needs, not a generic business checklist.
