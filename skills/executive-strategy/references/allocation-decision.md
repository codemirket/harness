# Worked allocation decision: attractive revenue, scarce delivery capacity

This is a fictional decision exercise, not a company forecast or financial advice.
Every amount and constraint below is an explicit illustrative assumption. Use the
method only after replacing these assumptions with the actual business evidence.

## Define the choice and units

Fieldwork Studio has EUR 300,000 of unrestricted cash at the start of month 1.
Its existing operations consume EUR 30,000 net cash each month after existing
collections and expenses. Existing payroll is included in that baseline. The
owner wants at least EUR 150,000 at every month-end during the next three months
and has 10 unallocated engineering person-weeks after existing commitments.
Neither the cash floor nor protected commitments may be waived by this analysis.

Three opportunities compete for the same period:

| Option | Customer hypothesis | Extra cash assumptions | Engineering capacity |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Repair core onboarding | Removing observed setup failures lets existing prospects activate | Pay EUR 40,000 in month 1; incremental net cash contribution of EUR 0 / 8,000 / 16,000 in months 1 / 2 / 3 | 8 person-weeks |
| Enterprise customization | One prospective buyer pays for a specialized workflow | Pay EUR 90,000 in month 1; collect EUR 40,000 in month 2 and EUR 80,000 in month 4; extra delivery/support payments EUR 8,000 in each of months 2 and 3 | 14 person-weeks |
| Partner distribution test | A partner can introduce qualified buyers economically | Pay EUR 25,000 in month 1; incremental net cash contribution of EUR 0 / 8,000 / 12,000 | 4 person-weeks |

Upfront payments are incremental external/one-time costs and exclude existing
payroll. Incremental contribution is cash received less incremental variable
payments in the stated month; it is not gross sales or recognized revenue. The
enterprise collection schedule is a proposed commercial assumption, not a signed
contract. The later EUR 80,000 cannot pay month-3 obligations. No tax, financing,
interest, currency conversion or further post-month-3 cash flows are modeled.
These omissions limit the model; obtain them if material before committing.

The fictional evidence packet has six observed onboarding failures among 20
eligible attempts, a proposal from one enterprise prospect, and partner estimates
without observed qualified conversions. These support different confidence levels:
an observed workflow problem, one prospective purchase, and an untested channel.
They do not establish the cash contributions assumed above.

## Reconcile cash and feasibility before ranking

For each month: closing cash = opening cash + baseline net flow + incremental
collections - incremental payments. Carry each closing balance into the next month.

| Option | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 | Fits 10 person-weeks? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Maintain current course | EUR 270,000 | EUR 240,000 | EUR 210,000 | Yes |
| Core onboarding | EUR 230,000 | EUR 208,000 | EUR 194,000 | Yes, 2 remain |
| Enterprise | EUR 180,000 | EUR 182,000 | EUR 144,000 | No, short by 4 |
| Partner test | EUR 245,000 | EUR 223,000 | EUR 205,000 | Yes, 6 remain |

Enterprise fails two independent constraints even though its headline proposed
sales are highest. Counting the month-4 collection now, or dividing total cash by
average burn, hides the month-3 gap. Outsourcing four person-weeks is not a free
repair: price, quality, onboarding time and cash timing would need a new scenario.
Core plus partner needs 12 person-weeks, so they cannot simply be combined under
the stated plan. Unused person-weeks are optionality, not uncounted cash income.

## Test what could reverse the recommendation

If all core and partner incremental contributions are zero, month-3 cash is
EUR 170,000 and EUR 185,000 respectively. Both stay above the illustrative floor,
but this three-month check does not establish eventual payback or profitability.
If the enterprise month-2 collection is delayed beyond month 3, month-3 cash is
EUR 104,000. A larger deposit alone cannot repair the capacity constraint.

To satisfy just the enterprise month-3 cash floor with other assumptions fixed,
at least EUR 46,000 must be collected by month 3: 300,000 - 90,000 baseline burn
- 90,000 upfront - 16,000 support + 46,000 = 150,000. The timing must also preserve
the floor at each earlier checkpoint. This is a feasibility threshold, not a
recommended price or proof of buyer willingness to pay.

For the core option, an additional EUR 20,000 of upfront cost exhausts its
zero-contribution month-3 headroom: 300,000 - 90,000 - 60,000 = 150,000. That makes
scope control consequential. Review costs before ordering work; do not treat the
threshold as an invitation to spend the remaining headroom.

## Make the strategic call without pretending the model decides it

Recommend a bounded core-onboarding repair, conditional on the owner confirming
that activation for the existing segment is the priority. It addresses observed
friction and fits both constraints even with zero short-term incremental cash.
Its strongest objection is that the partner test costs less and preserves more
capacity. The reason to prefer core is the stronger problem evidence and the
plausible dependency that newly acquired prospects also need reliable onboarding,
not the modeled cash totals, which actually favor the partner test.

Defer enterprise implementation pending a credible capacity plan and collection
terms that satisfy the cash floor; do not promise a delivery date. Keep partner
discovery small and within separately confirmed remaining capacity. If the actual
onboarding failures do not affect the targeted buyers, or partner evidence shows
qualified demand that bypasses that workflow, reconsider the sequence.

Proposed next step: the product owner confirms the eligible journey and engineering
estimates a repair slice within the eight person-weeks and EUR 40,000 ceiling.
Record the initial 20 attempts as a small diagnostic sample, not a representative
activation baseline. Before expanding, inspect corrected journeys and reconcile
eligible attempts, failures and support effort over a comparable observation
window. Pause expansion if cash projections breach the floor or material new
failure modes appear. Define numerical outcome targets with the owner once the
measurement baseline is credible; do not manufacture them from this example.

The memo is a recommendation. Approved allocation, executed spend, repaired
behavior and measured customer outcome remain distinct later evidence.
