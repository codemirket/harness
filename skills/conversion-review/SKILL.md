# Conversion review

Establish the intended audience, acquisition channel, offer, primary action, and
observed problem. Reuse existing product context. If analytics are unavailable,
label findings as usability observations or hypotheses rather than measured loss.

## Trace the decision

- Follow the actual visitor journey from the entry promise to the confirmation.
  Check that the offer, price, eligibility, and expected outcome remain consistent.
- Identify whether visitors can understand what is offered, why it matters to
  them, what supports the claim, and what happens after the primary action.
- Inspect friction at the point it occurs: unclear labels, unnecessary fields,
  missing pricing or delivery information, validation errors, inaccessible controls,
  slow feedback, or lost work. Reproduce issues on relevant screen sizes.
- Keep evidence and interpretation separate. A short form may reduce effort but
  does not establish a universal conversion uplift or improve lead quality.
- Evaluate business and user outcomes together. Preserve informed choice, honest
  urgency, visible recurring terms, accessible errors, and a usable exit path.

## Recommend and verify

Prioritize a few changes by evidence, likely reach, implementation effort, and
reversibility. For each, show the observed problem, proposed change, intended
mechanism, and evidence that would confirm or reject the hypothesis.

Use real customer proof with permission and attribution; never invent testimonials,
scarcity, customer logos, or performance figures. Preserve the product's voice and
existing design system. Test changed flows through success and relevant failure
states. For a measured uplift claim, use a defensible experiment and guardrails
such as lead quality, refunds, retention, and support burden.
