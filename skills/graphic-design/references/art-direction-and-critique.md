# Art direction and visual critique

Use this for original illustration and substantial visual compositions. Scale
effort to the brief: a small brand correction needs neither a mood board nor an
approval ceremony. The aim is a clear visual idea and convincing execution.

## Establish the direction

Recover audience, message, placement and brand constraints from the task. State
the central visual idea in one sentence, then choose subject/action, viewpoint,
silhouette, focal hierarchy and a suitable medium. Decide which detail must
survive at the smallest size. Use actual copy and real asset proportions early.

When references help, compare several relevant examples for different reasons:
one may teach composition, another material/light, another typography or brand
fit. Include subject construction evidence when anatomy or machinery matters.
State what transfers and what does not. Do not average incompatible styles,
copy distinctive assets, or treat similarity to one reference as the score.
The brief and supplied brand decisions govern; references remain evidence.

When an important creative choice remains open, compare inexpensive,
materially different concepts. Change the idea, viewpoint or composition, not
only palette and decoration. Select for communication and placement fit before
polishing. Continue autonomously when the brief establishes the direction.

Start from concrete subject evidence: objects, materials, actions and meaningful
relationships. A metaphor must preserve the relationship it is meant to explain.
Make a focal specimen at the intended placement before developing a whole family.
If it needs a generated asset, establish that producer and inspect actual output;
a geometry placeholder does not prove that the eventual material will be convincing.
For a diagram, use [semantic construction](diagram-semantics.md) before layout.

Choose a brief-specific craft target that can be inspected. A glass vessel may
need a coherent rim, thickness and transmitted light; a character needs a readable
pose and credible attachments; a botanical plate needs recognizable structure.
Compare references for those separate responsibilities. Neither one reference's
style nor a generic "premium" score supplies the target.

## Critique the actual pixels

First inspect the whole composition at intended size, then a reduced view and
focal details. For a family, inspect the set together and individual crops. Use
the following lenses without turning them into a universal style prescription:

| Lens | Evidence to examine | A convincing failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept | The image conveys this message through subject and action | Attractive stock symbolism unrelated to the brief |
| Composition | Focal point, silhouette, negative space, hierarchy and reading order | Competing focal objects or a crop that erases the action |
| Construction | Anatomy/contact, perspective, light, materials and edge craft | A floating grip, impossible joint or inconsistent shadow |
| Coherence | Consistent intentional visual language across image, type and assets | Unrelated icon geometry or mismatched lighting in a composite |
| Placement fit | Brand, audience, small-size clarity, real background and crop | Beautiful isolated artwork that fails in the actual page |

Keep factual accuracy, accessibility, rights and file integrity as separate
constraints. A high craft score cannot cancel a false diagram or unreadable label.
Likewise, valid SVG, a clean diff and successful export cannot establish appeal.

For each material finding name the visible location, why it harms the brief,
and the next concrete edit. Distinguish a defect from a taste preference and
uncertainty. Avoid empty verdicts such as "premium", "polished" or "looks good".
Describe strengths too so a revision does not destroy what already works.

## Revise and stop deliberately

Fix the one to three highest-impact defects, render again, and compare the
affected region and complete composition. Changing medium or visual idea can
be more effective than polishing an unsuitable construction. Save a useful
previous version before a risky revision; do not retain endless near-duplicates.

Classify the failure before choosing a repair: missing subject specificity needs
an idea or evidence change; a weak silhouette needs recomposition; implausible
contact or material needs reconstruction; tiny placement needs a crop/detail
decision. Extra grain, gradients or motion cannot repair those causes. A simpler
surface can help hierarchy, but removing detail alone does not create substance.

Keep unchanged references available during revisions. In a Mirket-owned worker,
`inspect_image` accepts `purpose: reference`, `comparison` or `artifact`. Only two
references receive priority within four images/8 MiB total. Choose or prepare a
legible contact sheet when a larger set matters; reread any evicted pixels needed
for a decision. Preserved context never replaces fresh output verification.

For consequential work, use a separate critique pass; independent reviewers can
help when useful and authorized. Give reviewers the actual brief and pixels,
multiple relevant references if needed, and no flattering author narrative.
In comparisons, conceal condition labels and vary presentation order. Reviewers
must cite visible observations; an AI verdict is not user acceptance.

Stop when the brief is fulfilled, material defects are resolved and remaining
choices are preferences, or when the authorized budget is reached. If the budget
ends first, preserve the result and name unresolved defects. Do not silently
increase spending, select only the best trial, or describe the work as accepted.
