# Customer research

Define the decision the research should inform, the relevant customer segments,
and what is already known. Distinguish research planning, analysis of supplied
material, and contacting participants; outreach requires explicit authorization.

## Gather useful evidence

- Choose participants based on the decision. Include relevant non-buyers, lost
  customers, or unsuccessful users when success stories would bias the answer.
- Explain the purpose and use of participation honestly. Obtain appropriate
  consent for recording and attribution; collect only necessary personal data.
- Ask about specific recent behavior, context, alternatives, constraints, and
  decisions. Avoid leading questions, bundled questions, and hypothetical praise.
- Preserve the distinction between what someone did, what they said, and the
  researcher's interpretation. Keep quotations exact and traceable to the source.

## Synthesize without manufacturing certainty

Group patterns by segment and context. Record supporting observations,
counterexamples, and conflicting accounts. Separate repeated evidence from a
memorable anecdote; frequency in a convenience sample is not population prevalence.
Do not infer confidence or saturation from an arbitrary interview count.

Connect findings to the product or marketing decision. Describe unmet needs,
switching triggers, barriers, decision criteria, and the words participants use.
Label proposed messaging and product changes as hypotheses until tested.

For planning, deliver a sampling approach and question guide tied to the decision.
For synthesis, deliver an evidence map with source references, sampling limits,
alternative explanations, and the next useful uncertainty to resolve.
Redact private participant details from shared outputs and retain raw material
only within the agreed access and retention boundaries.
