# Venture validation

Turn the idea into a decision about the next useful commitment. Identify the
customer, painful job, current alternative, buyer, offer and route to that buyer.
Reuse evidence already available and distinguish a new venture from an extension
of an existing business with known customers and constraints.

## Locate the uncertainty that matters

Separate demand, willingness to pay, repeat usage, acquisition, delivery and
economic viability. Record the assumptions whose failure would change the
decision. Focus the next test on the most consequential unresolved assumption;
do not require a complete business plan before a small reversible test.

Treat behavior according to what it demonstrates:

- Interviews and complaints can establish a problem and vocabulary, but do not
  establish representative demand or purchases.
- Signups and letters of intent depend on qualification and commitment terms.
  Paid pilots show more commitment but may reflect a discount, founder service
  or one-off need rather than repeatable full-price demand.
- Continued use, renewals and referrals have a cohort and time window. Record
  cancellations, nonresponses and failed deliveries as well as successes.
- Simulated personas, a generated survey answer or an executive role-play are
  hypotheses; they are not customer evidence.

## Test a viable business, not just interest

Specify the unit sold, realized price, refund/discount terms, variable delivery
cost, support burden and acquisition cost. Distinguish gross revenue from
contribution and founder effort from a scalable cost model. Estimate payback
using cash timing and retention, and show what must hold for it to occur.
Do not infer lifetime value from immature cohorts or fund a cash gap with an
uncommitted investment assumption.

Use available market and financial analysis workflows when the decision needs
source-backed market reach or a cash forecast. For a paid-pilot decision, read
the [worked evidence and economics case](references/pilot-evidence.md).

## Design the next experiment

State the hypothesis, target segment, observable action, duration, cost/capacity
limit and decision rule before collecting results. Match the method to the
uncertainty: an interview, prototype, concierge delivery, price test or renewal
test can answer different questions. Keep selection bias, sample limitations
and alternative explanations visible. Do not claim statistical significance
from a convenient sample or move the success threshold after seeing results.

Prefer the smallest experiment that can change the decision. Avoid fake claims,
invented scarcity or collecting personal data unrelated to the test. Prepare a
concrete experiment within the user's scope; obtaining participants, sending
messages, charging money or buying traffic requires the relevant authorization.

## Decide and preserve learning

Compare outcomes to the predeclared rule and explain counterevidence. Recommend
continue, change or stop with the reason, exposure and next reversible action.
Keep measured results, interpretation and remaining assumptions distinct.
Do not turn a promising test into a guarantee of product-market fit or a mandate
to build the entire proposed product.
