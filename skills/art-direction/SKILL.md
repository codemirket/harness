# Art direction

Identify audience, message, medium, content hierarchy and the response the artifact should evoke. Inspect supplied references and assets; distinguish the qualities to learn from their protected brands, fonts and artwork. Do not substitute a generic visual trend for the actual brief.

When references would materially improve a direction, assemble a small project-owned context pack on demand. Use task-specific annotated crops rather than a moodboard: record the desired property, where it applies, the source/revision and rights, and an explicit do-not-copy/prohibited-copy note. Add the existing product tokens, components and constraints that the direction must preserve, plus owned good/bad cases when they clarify the target. Explain positive subject, material, typography and motion relationships; do not turn a reference into arbitrary style bans or copied source prose.

For an existing project, name the route, component, token, font, copy and asset owners you will preserve, and state the exact files or artifacts in scope before editing. Honor a user-authorized scope without asking again; ask only when a new deletion, broader replacement or other destructive scope is ambiguous.

Keep generated previews, logs, browser profiles and caches inside the project or explicitly authorized scratch directory. A temporary path is still a write destination; do not assume `/tmp` is in scope. If a permitted renderer cannot run within the host's permissions, retain the source and report the untested view rather than widening the write scope.

For external assets actually incorporated into the artifact, retain the creator, source, applicable license and required attribution/notices with the deliverable. A reference library's prose license does not license its fonts, photographs or branded artwork.

Choose a coherent direction across composition, type, color, image treatment, density and motion where requested. Explain the few choices that make it distinct and suited to the content. Use real copy and available assets early. For a public-facing page, map each claim to supplied or verified proof; never invent metrics, testimonials, logos or case-study results. Use a labelled placeholder or change the structure when proof is missing, and mark illustrative sample details for confirmation. Produce a minimal native specimen or permitted raster composition and inspect its rendered result before expanding the system.

Rendering a PNG, validating SVG/XML or checking dimensions does not show the pixels to the reviewer. Claim visual inspection only after an image-capable tool actually displays the saved render at the target size. If that view is unavailable, report visual QA as untested and keep composition, legibility and clipping judgments provisional; do not infer them from source or a successful render command.

Find a visual idea in the subject: its objects, process, setting, audience or language. For an open-ended brief, inventory a short set of facts, places, materials, rituals, constraints or artifacts specific to the subject. State what subject fact becomes which compositional device. Test whether replacing the subject with a known competitor or another subject would leave the design essentially unchanged; do not invent competitor facts or require unrelated research for a self-contained brief. A palette or list of style adjectives is not yet a direction. Decide what attracts attention first, what carries the message and how supporting detail earns its space. Judge the focal artwork at the size and crop where it will actually appear.

When a costly direction is unresolved, compare a small number of structurally different specimens at matching fidelity and content. Change the visual argument, such as image-led storytelling versus a dense editorial opening, rather than generating color variants. Record why the chosen direction carries the subject-specific idea and why any redraw was needed; do not redraw merely for comfort. Reuse an accepted reference or direction without reopening exploration. Keep supplied brand, copy and accessibility constraints visible while choosing.

Freeze that specimen as a comparison target. Check whether the focal point, reading order, contrast and material character survive actual dimensions and relevant viewport changes. Let evidence support a target repair before expanding; preserve an explicit user's chosen direction.

Choose the production medium from the deliverable. Use native DOM for a working screen, editable vector source for geometry, design-file structure for an editable mockup and available host image generation for a bitmap composition that benefits from it. A raster target can guide implementation; it does not establish responsive layout or interaction. Verify the actual qualified Mirket route before claiming a design operation.

For optional comparison, the approved catalog retains pinned [Hallmark](https://github.com/Nutlope/hallmark/tree/13ac0ec7e148655948100b6396439e481361d690) and [Taste](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill/tree/f624f37a8c650b9cc550253104925efe4c8de246) sources. Consult only a portion that resolves the current concept question. These are comparison locators; they do not install a workflow or grant reuse rights to text, artwork or fonts.

Deliver the brief, chosen direction, subject-specific idea, claim/proof notes when relevant, inspected target and reasons for major choices. Before the final report, render the **final saved artifact**, not only its seed or specimen. Record the final render path and the image-capable view that displayed it. If that view did not occur, state "final visual inspection untested"; do not say the image was visually inspected, legible or unclipped. A successful renderer command or XML check supports only those narrower claims.

Stop once the direction is concrete enough to guide production, or report the missing asset/decision that prevents it. Unless the task sets another budget, share 15 minutes/two final repairs for static or vector work, 30 minutes/three for interfaces or paged work, and 45 minutes/four for motion. Combined work uses the largest limit, not their sum; allow one target repair and report defects at the limit. A new specimen does not reset it.
