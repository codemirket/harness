# Casa da Música identity — variation derived from a subject

Study language: **Subject-derived variable identity**. This label is a curator interpretation, not an upstream standard name.

## Brief → concept → grammar

**Brief.** Give Fourfold, a fictional paper-arts programme, a variable identity for printing, construction and repair sessions. The variation must arise from the programme’s subject rather than a random polygon generator.

**Concept.** One accordion-folded sheet can be seen differently without becoming a different object. Four connected panels and their three folds link the sessions through an ordinary, meaningful material.

**Grammar.** Sagmeister’s Casa da Música case study derives multiple identity views from the music centre’s architecture, connecting one house with many musical characters. The transferable relationship is subject → form → varied application. Fourfold invents a different subject, a paper fold; it does not use the building’s silhouette, facets, proportions or source logo generator.

## Located decisions in the original study

- `.source-sheet` introduces the original object before its applications. Panel numbers 1–4 and the caption expose its connectivity.
- Every correct `.programme svg` is an orthographic projection of one set of five fold-edge positions, extruded to a fixed sheet height. The view angles are −35°, +10° and +35°; the source view is −20°. Panel 2 always neighbours panels 1 and 3.
- `.panel[data-panel]` preserves the four-panel relationship with actual SVG polygons and shared edges. It is editable vector source; a label saying “generative” would not substitute for this construction.
- The three poster backgrounds locate programme variation. Labels preserve the session meanings. Colour is intentionally assigned here; there is no claim to reproduce or verify the source’s colour-sampling algorithm.

## Attractive wrong variant and discriminating failure

Open `?variant=misleading`. Two posters still derive from the sheet; the third becomes an attractive arrangement of five independent facets. Palette, framing and faceted appearance remain coherent, but the shape cannot be a view of the four-panel source.

Trace panels 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 and count the shared folds in every application. Reject the misleading final poster: there is no continuous sheet or three-fold relationship. Merely recognising a common aesthetic, or finding a familiar silhouette, cannot pass this test.

## Transfer to a different brief

Create a visual family for a local woven-basket exhibition. Study one actual weaving structure, derive distinct views or crops that retain its over-under relationship, and connect those variations to real programme themes. Show the source construction and test a crop at a small use size. Do not replace the source study with random facets or reuse Fourfold’s paper object.

**Suitable:** cultural programmes, exhibitions, subject-rich institutions. **Poor fit:** arbitrary novelty, a functional sign that needs one fixed decoding, or context colour used as the only category identifier.
