# MIT Media Lab identity — variation inside a shared construction

Study language: **Constrained generative identity**. This label is a curator interpretation, not an upstream standard name.

## Brief → concept → grammar

**Brief.** Give three collections in the fictional Common Field Archive recognisable peer identities, usable on a large collection panel and a small specimen label. The collections are Sound records, Material samples and Field notes.

**Concept.** Each collection gets one distinctive route through a common field. Related routes express institutional kinship; repeating the same route beside the same name establishes identity over time.

**Grammar.** Pentagram’s 2014 case study describes the ML monogram and 23 research-group glyphs on a seven-by-seven grid. The lesson is bounded invention with stable mapping. This original study instead uses a polar lattice: six rays, three rings and one open route of shared optical weight. It has no ML letters, seven-by-seven cells or traced group marks.

## Located decisions in the original study

- The once-defined SVG symbols `#cf-sound`, `#cf-matter` and `#cf-field` are three new paths on the same polar field. All subsequent instances use those definitions; there are no duplicate gradient or mask IDs.
- `main.archive .family` presents the named master identities as equal peers. The names are essential HTML, not encoded only in the unfamiliar marks.
- `#construction` reveals the shared rings and rays with a native checkbox and CSS. The marks and names exist before scripting and remain complete without it.
- `.labels .tag` repeats the same marks at a smaller size. The correct `data-entity` and `data-mark` assignments match what is actually drawn; these attributes are inspectable evidence, not proof by themselves.

## Attractive wrong variant and discriminating failure

Open `?variant=misleading`. All three routes still obey the polar field and share weight, palette and finish. But the small labels rotate their assignment: Sound records uses Material samples’ route, and the other two are similarly reassigned.

Choose one master mark and find its specimen label by shape. The correct version preserves that identity; the misleading version contradicts it. A test that only checks grid membership or visual consistency cannot discriminate the error. The named repeat, not an aesthetic family resemblance alone, is the acceptance boundary.

## Transfer to a different brief

Make four identities for a community seed exchange. Choose a new construction grounded in its organisation, define which features stay fixed and which encode each collection, then repeat each named mark on a packet and a notice. Test stable mapping and small-scale recognition. Do not copy these archive routes or equate an abstract identity mark with an instruction icon.

**Suitable:** federated programmes, research collections, campaign families. **Poor fit:** sole labels for urgent actions, emergency status, or functional icons that require immediate unfamiliar decoding.
