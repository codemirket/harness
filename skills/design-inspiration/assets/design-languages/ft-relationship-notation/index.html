Fieldnotes / analytical graphics · illustrative data

First the question.
Then the notation.

A count, a spread and a transfer ask different things of a chart. A shared visual voice can hold them together without pretending they are the same relationship.

01 / DEVIATION

Who is above the target?

Show signed distance from a meaningful reference. Zero divides surplus and shortfall.

Measure: percentage points from target

A share-of-whole form cannot encode signed distance.

Fictional targets. Negative and positive values share one baseline; length measures the same unit.

02 / DISTRIBUTION

How long do people wait?

A median alone hides the spread. Count observations in equal, stated time bins.

Grain: 20 completed visits

A connecting line suggests a progression through time, even though the values describe different duration bins.

Bin boundaries and counts are explicit. The horizontal axis is waiting duration, not calendar time.

03 / FLOW

Where does the material go?

Preserve a quantity through a transfer. The two outgoing streams must reconcile to the incoming total.

Unit: kilograms in one batch

Original simplified flow diagram. Stream widths represent kilograms; reuse plus recycling equals the 100 kg input. There are no unobserved losses.
