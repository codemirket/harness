A small paper experiment · fictional teaching essay

One sheet.
Many layers.

A fold makes the footprint smaller. It also makes the stack thicker. The same sheet tells both stories.

Start with a square. Fold it in half, then in half again. Each fold doubles the number of layers while halving the visible area.

The surprise is a relationship: twice the layers, half the footprint. A beautiful crease is only useful here if it helps explain that tradeoff.

Fold, then compare.

4 layersoccupy 25% of the original footprint.

The whole sheet is still here.

Nothing has been removed. In this simplified model, layers × footprint always equals one original sheet. Looking at thickness alone would miss half the explanation.

  • 0 folds: 1 layer, 100% area
  • 1 fold: 2 layers, 50% area
  • 2 folds: 4 layers, 25% area
  • 3 folds: 8 layers, 12.5% area
  • 4 folds: 16 layers, 6.25% area

This is an arithmetic model, not a prediction of the maximum folds possible with a real sheet.

Language: subject-led visual explanation.

The fold generates the title treatment, diagram, local control and reading sequence. Its invariant—one sheet—remains visible while the footprint changes. This transfers a relationship studied in The Pudding’s onion essay; the subject, prose and artwork here are original.
