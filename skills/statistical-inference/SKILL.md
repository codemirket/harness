# Statistical inference

Identify the estimand, target population, sampling/assignment process and unit of independence. Check whether the data and study design can support the requested claim. Distinguish randomized, observational and descriptive evidence before choosing an estimator or test.

Inspect missingness, censoring, repeated measures, clustering, outliers and model assumptions that can change uncertainty. Choose an analysis appropriate to that structure; do not use a familiar test merely because it returns a p-value. Account for multiple comparisons/looks and any post-hoc choice.

Report effect magnitude, units and interval/uncertainty alongside relevant diagnostics. Assess practical meaning and sensitivity to consequential assumptions. A statistically detectable association is not automatically causal, representative or useful.

Return reproducible analysis, design/assumption checks and a conclusion calibrated to evidence. Stop at an unsupported estimand or missing independence/design information; describe the limit instead of manufacturing precision. Do not change the success criterion after seeing results without labeling the exploratory analysis.
