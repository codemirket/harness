# Jobs to be done

Identify the situation that triggers a person to seek progress and the outcome they are trying to achieve. Ground the job in actual customer evidence rather than the product's feature vocabulary. Distinguish functional, social and emotional dimensions only where the sources support them.

Map the current workaround/alternative and the forces affecting change: pressure from the current situation, appeal of the new option, uncertainty and habits/constraints. Preserve differences across circumstances and segments. A tidy job sentence does not establish demand or willingness to pay.

Use the framing to compare solution directions and the evidence that could discriminate them. Keep hypotheses explicit and avoid turning every feature into a separate universal job.

Return an evidence-linked job/forces map, alternatives and the next consequential uncertainty. Stop when the framing clarifies a real decision or report that customer circumstances are missing. Do not invent purchase stories or claim validation from the phrasing alone.
