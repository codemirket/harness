# Financial analysis

Answer the business decision with a reconciled model, explicit assumptions and
the conditions that change the conclusion. Scale the model to that decision;
an operating budget does not automatically need a valuation or three statements.

## Establish the basis

- Read the supplied accounts, contracts and existing model. Identify the entity,
  reporting period, currency, units, accounting basis and decision horizon.
  Respect the declared reporting calendar. If periods are omitted, establish the
  treatment of intervening flows; do not silently expand or reject the calendar.
- Separate actuals, signed commitments, estimates and scenarios. Keep material
  inputs traceable to a dated source and distinguish missing data from zero.
- Identify restricted cash, minimum liquidity, debt maturities, collection delays
  and committed spending when assessing solvency or funding needs. Do not assume
  proposed financing arrives merely because a forecast needs it.
- Verify current rates, accounting treatment or jurisdictional requirements when
  material. State an unresolved treatment; do not invent a tax or legal conclusion.

## Model the economics and the cash

- Build from business drivers: customers or units, realized price, retention,
  delivery costs, people and timing. Keep invoicing, recognized revenue and cash
  receipts distinct. Explain whether a number is bookings, GMV, revenue or margin.
- Separate recurring revenue from one-time revenue. ARR is an annualized recurring
  run rate on a stated date, not the revenue earned during the preceding year.
  Define the customer and revenue denominators before applying retention rates.
- Reconcile opening cash + receipts - payments + net financing = closing cash.
  Include investment, principal repayments, working capital and taxes where
  relevant. Explain the bridge from earnings to cash rather than treating profit
  as available funds.
- Define burn signs explicitly. A positive cash deficit may be called net burn;
  a cash-generating period has no positive burn-based runway. Cash divided by a
  historical average is only a rough estimate under stable future cash flows.
  For changing flows, find the first period the forecast breaches required cash.
  Month-end balances do not establish liquidity within the month. Qualify funding
  amounts and arrival deadlines by the model's time resolution; use transaction
  dates or a clearly labeled timing bound before claiming payments can be met.
- For unit economics, define the unit, cohort and time window. Include refunds,
  fees, variable fulfilment and attributable acquisition costs as appropriate.
  Revenue is not contribution, and contribution is not cash collected. Do not
  extrapolate lifetime value from an immature cohort without exposing that limit.

## Stress the decision

Use a plausible downside tied to the business: slower collections, lower demand,
churn, delivery overruns or delayed financing. Change connected assumptions
coherently and show the cash effect, operating response and reversal condition.
Label scenarios by their assumptions; P10/P50/P90 requires a probability model,
not three hand-picked columns. Avoid presenting an arbitrary buffer or industry
benchmark as the user's risk tolerance.

For a cash-versus-profit or runway analysis, read the
[worked cash case](references/cash-and-earnings.md). For a workbook deliverable,
use an available spreadsheet workflow for formula, recalculation and file checks.

## Verify and deliver

Check period boundaries, signs, units, opening/closing balances, source totals,
roll-forwards and changed-input behavior. Reconcile linked statements when the
task requires them. Review zero volume, delayed collection and a material
downside rather than adding a fixed checklist unrelated to the model.

Lead with the supported decision, liquidity constraint or unresolved gap. Provide
the model, assumptions, sensitivity and enough source links to audit the result.
Keep observed results distinct from forecasts and explain the practical limit
of the checks performed. Analysis does not authorize payments, borrowing,
investment trades, filings or changes to accounting records.
