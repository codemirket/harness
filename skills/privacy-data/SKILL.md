# Privacy and data lifecycle

Map what data is collected/derived, why, under whose authority, in which scope and where it flows. Identify durable stores, caches, logs, backups, exports and third parties separately. Verify current legal/regulatory claims against appropriate sources when they affect the decision.

Minimize data and access to the stated purpose. Keep credentials/private content out of ordinary receipts and logs. Define retention, consent withdrawal, correction, forgetting and restore behavior with an owner for each copy. User-owned external exports have a different control boundary from managed backups.

Exercise the actual lifecycle with representative disposable data: denied/unselected scope, correction, deletion, interrupted cleanup and restore from an older managed backup. Verify derived indexes/caches do not resurrect forgotten content. Do not claim deletion everywhere when some copies cannot be inspected or controlled.

Return the data-flow/lifecycle contract, verified effects and residual copies/access limits. Stop at missing authority or an irreversible unapproved deletion; preserve evidence without retaining unnecessary private content. A deletion receipt alone does not prove all governed copies were removed.
