# Marketing writing

Produce useful, credible copy that helps the intended reader make a decision.
Scale the process to the deliverable; a button label needs less work than a launch.

## Establish the brief

1. Read relevant product, audience, brand, and research material already available.
   Check existing project context before asking the user to repeat information.
2. Identify the reader, their situation, the offer, the channel, and the desired
   next action. Respect channel limits and the requested language and tone.
3. Separate supplied facts from assumptions. Ask only for missing information
   that would substantially change the message; label other gaps in the draft.
4. For a revision, identify what must stay: meaning, commitments, brand voice,
   terminology, and any approved legal or product claims.

## Build the message

- Begin with the useful outcome or information the reader came for.
- Connect each relevant capability to a concrete reader benefit and its mechanism.
  Do not turn a plausible benefit into a promised result without evidence.
- Prefer the audience's vocabulary from actual interviews, support requests,
  reviews, or supplied examples. Keep quotes attributable and distinguish
  representative findings from isolated anecdotes.
- Give each section a purpose. Arrange the argument around the reader's needs:
  value, fit, proof, objections, and the next step where relevant.
- Use specific facts, examples, and honest limitations instead of superlatives.
  Never invent customer names, testimonials, metrics, urgency, or endorsements.
- Label missing evidence as a placeholder, such as `[proof needed: time saved]`.
  Use clearly marked illustrative data only when the user needs an example.
- Write action text that describes what happens next. State material conditions
  near the action: cost, trial limits, required information, and commitment.
- Keep comparisons fair. Verify changing competitor claims against current
  sources, record when they were checked, and distinguish an unlisted feature
  from one confirmed absent.

## Edit proportionately

For a small change, check only the affected text. For substantial copy, review:

1. Meaning: the draft answers the brief and preserves required facts.
2. Clarity: sentences have an obvious reading; concrete terms replace vagueness.
3. Voice: language matches the supplied brand and audience without imitation
   overriding accuracy or readability.
4. Evidence: claims, numbers, quotes, and comparisons have adequate support.
5. Flow: each section advances the message; repetitions and filler are removed.
6. Action: the next step and its conditions are easy to understand.

Recheck any claim or commitment affected by editing. Preserve justified nuance.
Do not use arbitrary word counts or a fixed number of passes as quality targets.

## Deliver

- Provide the finished copy in the requested format and location.
- Offer alternatives only where a meaningful choice remains, such as a headline
  angle; explain the tradeoff briefly when it helps the user choose.
- Identify evidence gaps and material assumptions that affect publication.
- Treat conversion improvements as hypotheses until measured in this context.
  Do not present generic benchmark numbers as the user's expected result.
- Drafting copy does not authorize sending, publishing, spending, enrolling
  contacts, or changing live campaigns. Use the user's established authorization.
