# Marketing writing

## Professional stance and collaboration

Own the reader's understanding of the offer and motivation to act. Ask why
this audience would care now, what they might reasonably doubt, and what the next
action actually commits them to. Build persuasion from value, mechanism and proof.
Prefer a truthful narrower promise over a stronger unsupported one; revise the
angle when audience evidence shows that the intended benefit is irrelevant or
misunderstood.

Challenge product language that inventories features without explaining their
use, and brand or growth ideas that hide conditions. Preserve the brand's voice
while making meaning easy to grasp. Hand off finished channel-ready copy, claim
support and any material proof gap; for experiments, explain the differing
hypotheses behind variants instead of offering cosmetic alternatives.


Produce useful, credible copy that helps the intended reader make a decision.
Scale the process to the deliverable; a button label needs less work than a launch.

For anti-slop or generic-copy refinement, use
[editorial preservation](references/editorial-preservation.md). Replace empty
emphasis with supported meaning while retaining voice, conditions and uncertainty;
do not invent specificity or apply a word blacklist.

For a broader marketing request, select the actual outcome before writing copy.
Use `customer-research` for audience evidence, `market-analysis` for market sizing,
`search-visibility` for organic discovery, `conversion-review` for funnel repairs
and `experiment-design` for causal tests. For channel operations, inspect the
`marketing-strategy`, `marketing-growth` or `marketing-community` profile with
`mirket catalog profiles`, then read and select only the relevant skill. A copy
draft is not a completed launch, email program, pricing analysis or channel plan.

## Establish the brief

1. Read relevant product, audience, brand, and research material already available.
   Check existing project context before asking the user to repeat information.
2. Identify the reader, their situation, the offer, the channel, and the desired
   next action. Respect channel limits and the requested language and tone.
3. Separate supplied facts from assumptions. Ask only for missing information
   that would substantially change the message; label other gaps in the draft.
4. For a revision, identify what must stay: meaning, commitments, brand voice,
   terminology, and any approved legal or product claims.

When a positioning brief exists, carry its audience, actual alternatives,
supported value, source locators, conditions and excluded claims into the copy.
If the position itself is unresolved, use `market-analysis` and its
`references/positioning-evidence.md` via
`mirket catalog read market-analysis --path references/positioning-evidence.md`.
Keep research with that owner; routine
copy refinement preserves the accepted position. A convincing value chain does
not authorize upgrading a proposed benefit into a measured customer result.

## Build the message

- Begin with the useful outcome or information the reader came for.
- Connect each relevant capability to a concrete reader benefit and its mechanism.
  Do not turn a plausible benefit into a promised result without evidence.
- Prefer the audience's vocabulary from actual interviews, support requests,
  reviews, or supplied examples. Keep quotes attributable and distinguish
  representative findings from isolated anecdotes.
- Give each section a purpose. Arrange the argument around the reader's needs:
  value, fit, proof, objections, and the next step where relevant.
- Use specific facts, examples, and honest limitations instead of superlatives.
  Never invent customer names, testimonials, metrics, urgency, or endorsements.
- Label missing evidence as a placeholder, such as `[proof needed: time saved]`.
  Use clearly marked illustrative data only when the user needs an example.
- Write action text that describes what happens next. State material conditions
  near the action: cost, trial limits, required information, and commitment.
- Keep comparisons fair. Verify changing competitor claims against current
  sources, record when they were checked, and distinguish an unlisted feature
  from one confirmed absent.

## Carry claims into the visual composition

For a landing page, campaign or film, pair each key claim with the actual proof
it can show: product state, source artifact, attributable result or clearly
illustrative scene. Give the visual owner the claim, evidence locator, conditions,
section/shot purpose and intended action. If proof is missing, narrow the claim
or mark the gap; a generated image cannot supply customer evidence.

Derive brand voice from supplied examples and counterexamples: sentence rhythm,
terminology, degree of formality and things the brand would not claim. A persona
can be an explicit writing lens, never a substitute for audience research or an
endorsement by the real person it resembles. Check the composed page or frame:
small captions, cropping and motion can remove conditions that were clear in copy.

## Edit proportionately

For a substantial revision, read [editorial preservation](references/editorial-preservation.md)
for claim-preserving cuts, concrete phrasing and worked examples. Use the relevant
example when a shorter edit has a similar risk; a label change needs no full review.

For a small change, check only the affected text. For substantial copy, review:

1. Meaning: the draft answers the brief and preserves required facts.
2. Clarity: sentences have an obvious reading; concrete terms replace vagueness.
3. Voice: language matches the supplied brand and audience without imitation
   overriding accuracy or readability.
4. Evidence: claims, numbers, quotes, and comparisons have adequate support.
5. Flow: each section advances the message; repetitions and filler are removed.
6. Action: the next step and its conditions are easy to understand.

Recheck any claim or commitment affected by editing. Preserve justified nuance.
Do not use arbitrary word counts or a fixed number of passes as quality targets.

## Deliver

- Provide the finished copy in the requested format and location.
- Offer alternatives only where a meaningful choice remains, such as a headline
  angle; explain the tradeoff briefly when it helps the user choose.
- Identify evidence gaps and material assumptions that affect publication.
- Treat conversion improvements as hypotheses until measured in this context.
  Do not present generic benchmark numbers as the user's expected result.
- Drafting copy does not authorize sending, publishing, spending, enrolling
  contacts, or changing live campaigns. Use the user's established authorization.
