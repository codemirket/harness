# Preserve the claim while improving the copy

Use for substantial copy refinement or a consequential edit to a claim. Start
with the requested editorial scope: critique, light revision, restructuring or a
new draft. If the user wants a critique, give specific findings before replacing
their prose. Keep strong original sentences and recognizable voice when revising.

## Identify what the sentence commits to

Before cutting or strengthening a claim, identify its actor, action and scope;
numbers and units; timeframe; conditions and exceptions; uncertainty; and promised
next step. Use the source material to resolve an ambiguity. Shorter wording is
not an improvement if it changes who receives a benefit or how certain it is.

Words such as “may,” “approximately,” “reported” and “in the pilot” can carry
evidence. A passive construction can accurately leave an unknown actor unnamed.
Inspect these choices for meaning before removing them. If sources disagree,
retain the uncertainty or flag the discrepancy rather than smoothing it away.

Keep approved terms, quotations, required notices and fixed action labels intact
unless their revision is in scope. If they appear inaccurate, identify the issue
and source rather than quietly replacing the agreed commitment.

## Replace empty emphasis with useful meaning

Ask what the reader learns from a sentence. A claim that could describe unrelated
products unchanged often needs a mechanism, relevant example or precise scope.
Use what is actually known; do not invent specificity to make the copy convincing.
When no useful fact is available, a direct sentence or a cut may be enough.

Keep deliberate rhythm and emphasis when they serve the audience. A familiar
phrase, contrast, short fragment or enthusiastic tone is not evidence of poor
writing by itself. Diagnose the particular sentence rather than applying a banned
word list, guessing authorship or assigning an unsupported quality score.

The following examples are invented editing exercises, not product claims.

### Preserve the evidence boundary

**Known:** In a pilot with 12 teams, eight reported spending less time preparing
weekly reviews. Preparation time was not measured.

**Draft:** “Our game-changing workspace saves every team hours each week.”

**Revision:** “Eight of the 12 pilot teams reported spending less time preparing
weekly reviews.”

The revision keeps the count, pilot scope and self-reported result. It cannot
promise a measured saving. A useful headline could instead describe a documented
workflow, but only if that mechanism is present in the supplied product material.

### Keep conditions beside the action

**Known:** The trial lasts 14 days, requires no payment card, and covers up to
three projects. The approved button label is “Start trial.”

**Draft:** “Get unlimited freedom to explore. Start trial.”

**Revision:** “Try up to three projects for 14 days. No payment card required.”
Keep the button “Start trial.”

The revision supplies material limits without adding a renewal promise or an
unsupported claim about access after the trial. Check the actual rendered action
area if placement is part of the task; correct copy elsewhere may still leave the
reader with the wrong expectation at the decision point.

### Retain voice while removing repetition

**Known:** A bakery's supplied voice is warm and conversational. Wednesday ordering
closes at noon; collection is Friday from 3 to 6 p.m.

**Draft:** “Leave room for Friday. Order by noon Wednesday to reserve a loaf for
Friday. Pick up Friday, 3–6 p.m.”

**Light revision:** “Leave room for Friday. Reserve your loaf by noon Wednesday.
Pick up Friday, 3–6 p.m.”

The opening still sounds like the bakery. The cut removes a repeated date while
preserving the deadline, action and collection window. “Order anytime” or “Ready
Friday morning” would change the offer. A request for a small edit does not call
for a new brand voice or an expanded campaign.

## Audit the revision against its source

Compare affected claims and action conditions after editing, especially numbers,
qualifiers, named actors and causal language. A plausible implication is still a
new claim. Check that moving text has not separated a material limit from the
benefit it qualifies or turned a quote into the company's own assertion.

Deliver the requested copy with only the evidence gaps or editorial decisions the
user needs. A brief note is useful when a persuasive sentence had to become more
qualified; a full editing log is rarely necessary. Preserve unresolved proof as
an explicit draft gap and distinguish clearer writing from measured conversion
improvement.
