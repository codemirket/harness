# Public and editorial page composition

Use for a public product page, landing page, portfolio or editorial surface. Begin
with the audience, the content they need and the actual next action. Brand, supplied
copy, assets and project constraints are authoritative. These patterns are options
for constructing a direction, not templates to apply to every page.

## Choose the narrative before arranging sections

Name what makes this page specific: a product demonstrated in use, a body of work,
a useful argument, a selection of items, or a service whose process explains its
value. Order content to answer the reader's questions. A landing page need not
include every familiar marketing section.

| Content and reader need | Composition to consider | When it is a poor fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand a working product | Product view with concise explanation, then a task-led walkthrough | No accurate product view exists and an invented mockup would misrepresent it |
| Judge a portfolio or collection | A legible index with representative work, consistent captions and deliberate featured items | Items are decorative placeholders rather than the thing being evaluated |
| Understand an argument or publication | Editorial sequence with readable measure, section hierarchy and evidence beside claims | The task is a quick transaction and prose delays the action |
| Evaluate a process or service | Sequence of stages, responsibilities and concrete outputs | The stages are invented to fill a layout |
| Decide from a comparison | Clear alternatives, relevant differences and a supported next action | Prices, claims or comparison data have not been supplied or verified |
| Recognize a visual brand or place | Strong representative imagery with restrained text and contextual detail | A generic stock image substitutes for the subject the user needs to see |

A hero establishes identity and a reason to continue; it need not occupy the whole
viewport. Choose its height and content from the statement, image and next action.
Keep the most relevant information visible on a common laptop viewport as well as
on a phone. Avoid solving a crowded fold by shrinking all text.

## Construct one visual direction

Describe the intended result in concrete terms, such as "clear product evidence,
quiet surfaces, confident sans headings, concise supporting copy" or "reading-led
page, warm paper roles, measured serif headings, generous pauses at topic changes."
Choose from the existing brand rather than accumulating unrelated effects.

- **Type roles.** Define display, section heading, body and caption roles. A
  contrasting display face can carry a public brand; one carefully tuned family
  can also work. Preserve supplied fonts and confirm asset availability before
  choosing a replacement. Use loaded weights and check fallback behavior.
- **Scale and wrapping.** Fit heading size to actual wording and available width.
  For a first pass, short display statements might use a fluid size from about
  2.5rem on narrow screens to 4–5rem on wide ones; an existing brand or a longer
  statement may need a different range. Inspect natural wraps rather than forcing
  a particular line count. Tune leading and tracking in the real face.
- **Measure.** Keep prose readable, initially around 55–75 characters where that
  suits the language and font. Let captions and wide comparison content have
  different measures. A giant heading and equally wide paragraph can flatten
  hierarchy rather than create confidence.
- **Color roles.** Use surface, primary text, supporting text, accent and state
  roles. A quieter supporting palette can let the subject lead; a strong brand
  color can cover more space when intentional and legible. Check actual foreground/
  background combinations, including links and focus, instead of applying a fixed
  accent-area quota.
- **Spacing rhythm.** Use smaller intervals within one thought and larger pauses
  between topics. Alternate a dense evidence region with a calmer explanation
  when the narrative calls for it. Consistent tokens can support varied rhythm.
- **Alignment and surfaces.** Align headings with the content they introduce or
  make a clear compositional reason for the offset. Use cards for distinct objects,
  panels or interactions; continuous prose may need only spacing and a rule.
  Symmetry, asymmetry, rounded corners and flat surfaces are all available choices.

Pick a few reinforcing decisions and carry them through navigation, body and closing
content. Do not change the whole brand to fix one cramped section. Sibling pages
should share the product system even when their content needs a different layout.

## Use evidence and assets deliberately

Show the thing the reader is deciding about. A real product capture may explain a
tool better than abstract art; actual work should lead a portfolio; genuine place
or product images matter when appearance is the purchase decision.

When assets are missing, use an obvious, labelled placeholder in a preview or an
appropriate image-generation capability when that serves the brief. State what
still needs an authentic asset. Do not treat an invented product screenshot, person,
customer logo or testimonial as evidence of a real product or endorsement.

For a chosen asset, decide the focal point, crop, aspect ratio and adjacent text
together. Preserve the subject at narrow widths. Avoid baking important copy into
an image when live text is needed. Reserve media dimensions to keep layout stable;
choose appropriate sizes and loading behavior through the project's existing
media pipeline. Verify rights and hosting requirements before production use.

Purposeful custom artwork can explain a workflow or reinforce a subject. Use the
project's existing vector/icon system for simple diagrams and symbols. A bitmap,
video or elaborate effect earns its cost through communication, not novelty alone.

## Navigation, actions and copy

Navigation should reflect real destinations and the audience's search for them.
Keep it compact when destinations are few; use an established accessible disclosure
pattern when several groups exist. A decorative navigation shape must still fit
labels, preserve keyboard use and expose destinations on mobile.

Choose a main action that accurately describes what happens. Place supporting
actions where they answer a different reader need, with appropriate emphasis. A
comparison section can have plan-specific actions; a long editorial article may
have no sales action. Do not add a second equally prominent button for symmetry.

Use concrete headings and body copy grounded in supplied facts. Demonstrations,
quotes and numbers should support nearby claims. If the user has supplied fixed
copy, improve composition around it; propose material copy changes explicitly
rather than silently manufacturing a more convenient message.

## Responsive composition and review

Inspect the opening fold and at least one lower section in the same render; a strong
hero can hide weak continuation. Check whether topic changes remain clear without
identical section padding or decorative labels everywhere.

At narrow widths, stack content in its intended reading order, recrop media and
adjust heading size/measure where needed. Do not remove essential evidence or an
action merely to make the screenshot clean. Check translated labels, text zoom,
long headings, missing images and supported themes. Use motion only when it helps
the content or an interaction; preserve readable content under reduced motion.

Compare the result against the intended direction and the reader's task: what is
noticed first, what establishes credibility, what invites the next action, and
which details feel unrelated. Refine specific observed problems without a mandatory
number of rounds or a self-awarded aesthetic pass score.

## Worked direction choices

**Developer product page.** The real product is easier to understand through a
request/response example than an abstract illustration. Keep the existing brand,
use a concise promise and accurate example near the opening action, then sequence
the main workflow with genuine product captures. Give code and explanatory prose
different measures; keep copy/select interactions usable. Inspect a long response
and mobile overflow. Avoid adding customer proof or performance numbers unless
they are provided or verified.

**Company portfolio.** The supplied work has distinct colors and image proportions.
Use restrained brand surfaces, consistent caption roles and an intentional featured
project so the work leads. Preserve useful image crops across widths and keep an
index of real projects. A varied grid can help composition, but a uniform grid is
better when readers primarily compare equivalent items.

**Editorial explanation.** The source is a long, structured article with figures.
Use a clear title/deck, readable body measure, meaningful section heads and figures
beside the claims they explain. Let the opening type carry identity without a
decorative hero image. Preserve citations, figure meaning and topic order; inspect
paragraph rhythm and figure captions at narrow widths and text enlargement.
