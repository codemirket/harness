# Connect positioning to customer evidence

Use when deciding how a product should be understood relative to the customer's
real alternatives. Keep the accepted position for routine copy work. Use the
existing brief or research record; this method needs no new workshop, scorecard
or fixed sample count. `market-analysis` owns the evidence and recommendation;
`marketing-writing` turns the resulting brief into channel-appropriate copy.

## Build the chain and challenge each link

Connect **actual alternatives → verified relative strengths → enabled value →
relevant segment → category expectations → proof for the proposed claims**.
Proof belongs beside every link as it is developed, not in a last-minute search
for justification. Record source URL or local locator, date, relevant passage or
observed task, population and uncertainty. Separate observation, customer report,
vendor claim and analyst inference.

| Link | What to establish | What could overturn it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual alternatives | What people used, considered or continued doing for this task, including manual work or no change where evidenced. Keep customer's alternatives separate from an analyst's competitor list. | Nonbuyers or lost deals chose an omitted workaround; a supposed competitor serves a different job or budget. |
| Relative strengths | A demonstrable capability or condition compared on like-for-like scope, configuration and terms. Inspect our product as well as current alternatives. | The advantage vanishes under a realistic workload, is available in another tier, or cannot be verified. Silence in public docs is not proof of absence. |
| Enabled value | Explain how the strength changes the customer's task and why it matters. Separate demonstrated mechanism from hypothesized outcome. | A new step, migration burden or failure mode consumes the benefit; observed customers do not care about it. |
| Relevant segment | Identify situations, constraints and behaviors that make the value useful. Include costs and cases poorly served, using successful, dissatisfied, churned and nonbuying evidence where available. | The apparent fit reflects only a selected happy-customer sample or a contractual exception. |
| Category expectations | Choose understandable language and examine the capabilities, budget, buyer and comparisons that it implies. An established category, narrower description or new frame each has costs. | People reasonably infer capabilities the product lacks, or the framing requires more explanation than it clarifies. |
| Claim and proof | Match each proposed claim to evidence of that exact scope, with qualifications and a recheck trigger. Carry unsupported outcomes as hypotheses. | A demo proves capability but is used as proof of savings; a testimonial is treated as a typical result; an old comparison no longer holds. |

A strength can matter relative to the customer's actual alternative without being
globally unique. Do not require an “only we” claim, assume every competitor lacks
it, or discard useful shared capabilities as irrelevant. Conversely, a unique
feature has no demonstrated customer value merely because it is rare.

Value explanations should preserve their causal gaps. A record history may let
reviewers retrieve decisions; it does not by itself prove fewer mistakes, saved
hours or automatic compliance. Theme count, agreement among staff, awards and
persuasive prose do not measure demand or positioning quality. Test uncertain
comprehension with `customer-research`; use `experiment-design` for causal claims.
Neither a new interview nor publication is authorized merely by using this method.

## Worked example: a useful strength without exclusivity

All organizations, records and results below are fictional teaching material.
Suppose a small supplier-onboarding product already has accepted scope: collect
documents, route review and retain a decision history. It cannot verify supplier
identity or approve compliance automatically.

- **Alternatives and sources:** interview transcript `BUY-07`, October 2,
  paragraphs 12–18, describes a shared spreadsheet and email attachments. Lost-deal
  note `LOSS-03`, October 3, records a buyer staying with an existing procurement
  suite. Retain both; the spreadsheet is not every buyer's alternative.
- **Relative capability:** replay `DEMO-04`, build `sample-1`, October 4, retrieves
  the document version tied to a review decision. The observed spreadsheet process
  needs a separate attachment search. The procurement suite also exposes history
  in inspected demo `COMP-02`; no exclusivity claim is supported. These observations
  establish retrieval mechanisms, not typical elapsed time or error reduction.
- **Value and fit:** retrieving the reviewed version addresses the concern in
  `BUY-07` about explaining prior decisions. The proposed segment is teams
  coordinating supplier documents by email without an established suite workflow.
  `LOSS-03` challenges expansion to suite customers because adopting another tool
  would duplicate work. Segment prevalence and commercial reach remain unmeasured.
- **Category:** “supplier document review” describes the supported task more
  precisely than “automated supplier compliance.” Treat this as a proposed frame;
  customer comprehension has not been tested, and scope must remain explicit.
- **Copy handoff:** “Keep supplier documents and their review decisions together”
  is supported by the fictional capability replay. “Cut onboarding time in half,”
  “the only audit-ready platform” and “automatic compliance” have no support here.
  Keep identity checks and approval responsibility in the product's stated limits.

The recommendation is a focused position for this workflow, with a cost: it does
not promise an end-to-end procurement system. Evidence of recurring attachment
confusion among actual suite users could reopen that segment decision. A changed
product build or competing offer should trigger rechecking the affected comparison,
not automatic redoing of every unaffected research item.

## Give the writer a usable brief

Provide the selected audience and situation, actual alternatives, value and
mechanism, category wording with implied expectations, source-supported claims,
material conditions, explicit exclusions and unresolved proof gaps. Distinguish a
recommendation from the user's accepted decision. Include counterevidence and the
finding that would change the position so later copy edits retain the boundary.
There is no need to restate this entire analysis in the public copy.
