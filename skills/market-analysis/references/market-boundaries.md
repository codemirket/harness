# Worked case: segment value and reachable sales

Use this fictional case to challenge segment-count shortcuts, duplicated leads
and annualized revenue presented as first-year revenue.

The defined market has 200 enterprise buyers with $100,000 annual addressable
spend each and 900 small-business buyers with $2,000 each. All prices share the
same currency, year and product scope. The product can currently serve only
enterprises.

- TAM: 200 × $100,000 + 900 × $2,000 = $21.8 million per year.
- SAM: 200 × $100,000 = $20 million per year.
- Serving one of two named segments does not make SAM 50% of TAM. Counting buyers
  instead of segment value would also use the wrong denominator.

Two proposed channels reach 120 and 80 eligible enterprise buyers, with 50 buyers
in both lists. Their union is 150, not 200. A modeled 20% win rate yields 30 new
customers before delivery constraints. The team can onboard 20 customers during
the period, so the plan cannot claim more than 20 on that delivery model.

At an assumed realized $100,000 annual recurring price, 20 new active customers
would produce $2 million in exit ARR. If all start on July 1, have identical
calendar-year service and no churn, first-year recognized revenue is $1 million.
An upfront annual collection would instead be $2 million of cash receipts.
Those are different measures, not three estimates of the same result.

The win rate and realized price remain assumptions until supported in this
segment and channel. The capacity bound is a ceiling, not a sales forecast.
Changing the start dates, collections or churn requires revising the relevant
measure rather than silently reusing the annualized number.
